Why these tools, and why in this order

CRMs come first (Weeks 1–4). Every automation you'll ever build for a client ends up reading from or writing to a CRM. Learning HubSpot first gives you clean vocabulary — contact, company, deal, property, pipeline, workflow — that transfers everywhere. GoHighLevel second, because once you know what a CRM is, GHL's sprawling all-in-one interface stops being overwhelming and starts looking like "a CRM plus a bunch of bolted-on channels."

Airtable is the addition I'd fight for (Week 5). It isn't in your original list, but in real VA work it's the piece that makes everything else possible: a place to put data that isn't a CRM record. Client request queues, content calendars, inventory, job trackers — automations need somewhere to read and write structured data, and spinning up a real database is out of scope for a VA. Airtable is that layer, and Zapier/Make/n8n all connect to it natively. One week, high payoff.

Connectors go simplest → hardest (Weeks 6–8, 10–11). Zapier teaches the mental model (trigger → action) with almost no friction. Make forces you to actually see data flowing through a scenario, which is where real understanding lives. n8n comes last because it assumes you already know both, and adds hosting, credentials, and JavaScript on top.

The AI layer sits between Make and n8n (Week 9). This is the second addition, and it's the one that makes you an "AI automation specialist" rather than an "automation VA." Knowing how to call an LLM API from inside a workflow — with a constrained prompt and structured JSON output — is the single highest-leverage skill on this list in 2026. It's placed after Make so you have an HTTP module to practice in, and before n8n so the n8n capstone can use it properly.

Lovable is the capstone (Week 12). Not because it's hardest, but because it's the track that produces something a client can look at. By then you'll have a CRM, a database, and a workflow engine to wire it into — so the app you build is a real front end on a real system, not a demo.

What I deliberately left out: Notion (overlaps Airtable for this use case), Slack/Discord bots (a notification step, not a skill), Bubble/Webflow (months of study for something Lovable now covers at VA scale), and Python (genuinely valuable, but it's a different career branch and would blow past 12 weeks).

Reach for the right tool

Where these tools overlap — and where they don't

Zapier, Make and n8n all do trigger/action automation. HubSpot and GHL are both CRMs. The money is in knowing which one to recommend, not in knowing where the buttons are.

CRM: HubSpot vs GoHighLevel

DimensionHubSpotGoHighLevel
Core ideaOne centralized contact record shared across sales, marketing and serviceAll-in-one agency stack: CRM + SMS + email + funnels + sites + automation
Pricing shapeFree tier, then per-seat and per-contact tiers that climb steeplyFlat fee, roughly $97–$297/mo, unlimited sub-accounts on the agency plan
Built forOne company, multiple teams needing alignment and clean reportingOne agency managing many client accounts at once
ReportingGenuinely strong — custom reports, attribution, forecastingFunctional but shallower; you'll export for serious analysis
Native SMS / phoneAdd-on, extra costBuilt in and central to the product
Websites / funnelsCMS is a separate paid hubIncluded funnel and site builder
You'd pitch it when…Client has a sales team, cares about data quality and reporting, and will grow into paid tiersClient is a local service business or you're running several clients and need one flat-fee platform

DimensionZapierMake.comn8n

Client handoffEasiest — clients can read a ZapModerate — the canvas needs a walkthroughHardest — you're effectively the maintainer
